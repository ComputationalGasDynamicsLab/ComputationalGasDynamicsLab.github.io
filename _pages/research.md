Our research develops **physics-based computational methods** for complex gas-dynamic and plasma phenomena, with an emphasis on kinetic theory, particle simulation, GPU-accelerated computing, and high-performance computing.

<div class="projects-intro">

<div class="projects-intro-label">RESEARCH PORTFOLIO</div>

<h2>Physics-Based Computational Science</h2>

<p>
We develop scalable simulation methods that connect fundamental nonequilibrium
physics with challenging problems in plasma processing, semiconductor manufacturing,
hypersonic aerothermodynamics, and scientific computing.
</p>

</div>

<div class="project-grid">

<div class="project-number">01</div>

<div class="project-category">KINETIC SIMULATION</div>

<h2>Comet</h2>

<h3>GPU-Accelerated Kinetic Simulation Framework</h3>

<p>
A GPU-accelerated kinetic simulation framework for particle-based
modeling of plasma and gas-dynamic systems.
</p>

<p>
Comet focuses on scalable particle simulation methods for predictive modeling
of complex nonequilibrium phenomena on modern high-performance computing systems.
</p>

<span>Kinetic Simulation</span>

<div class="project-category">SCIENTIFIC AI</div>

<h2>CometAI</h2>

<h3>AI-Assisted Scientific Computing</h3>

<p>
An AI-enabled workflow for assisting the development, configuration, execution,
and analysis of complex kinetic simulations.
</p>

<p>
CometAI combines scientific simulation with artificial intelligence to automate
computational workflows while maintaining physics-based modeling and high-fidelity
simulation capabilities.
</p>

<div class="project-number">03</div>

<div class="project-category">HYPERSONIC FLOW</div>

<h2>Hypersonic Nonequilibrium Flow</h2>

<h3>Kinetic Modeling of Hypersonic Aerothermodynamics</h3>

<p>
Particle-based modeling of rarefied and nonequilibrium flows encountered in
high-speed atmospheric entry and other extreme aerodynamic environments.
</p>

<p>
The research focuses on shock-layer physics, high-temperature nonequilibrium,
and predictive modeling of complex hypersonic aerothermodynamic phenomena.</p>

<div class="project-card">

<div class="project-number">04</div>

<div class="project-category">LOW-TEMPERATURE PLASMA</div>

<h2>Plasma-Enabled Manufacturing</h2>

<h3>Kinetic Modeling for Semiconductor Processing</h3>

<p>
Kinetic simulation of low-temperature plasmas used in advanced semiconductor
manufacturing, with emphasis on plasma transport and plasma-surface interactions.
</p>

<p>
The research examines the ion and electron distributions that control plasma
processing, including high-aspect-ratio etching and ion energy and angular distributions.
</p>

<div class="project-focus">

<strong>Research Focus</strong>

<span>RF Plasma</span>
<span>Plasma Transport</span>
<span>Plasma-Surface Interactions</span>
<span>Semiconductor Etching</span>
<span>Ion Energy Distributions</span>

</div>

<div class="project-card project-card-wide">

<div class="project-number">05</div>

<div class="project-category">HIGH-PERFORMANCE COMPUTING</div>

<h2>GPU-Accelerated Computing</h2>

<h3>Scalable Algorithms and Numerical Methods</h3>

<p>
Development of computational algorithms that enable large-scale particle
simulations on modern GPU-based high-performance computing systems.
</p>

<p>
Particular emphasis is placed on scalable numerical methods for unstructured
meshes, particle-field coupling, and efficient solution of the field equations
required by kinetic particle simulations.
</p>

<div class="project-focus">

<strong>Research Focus</strong>

<span>GPU Algorithms</span>
<span>Unstructured-Mesh Simulation</span>
<span>Particle-Field Coupling</span>
<span>Charge Conservation</span>
<span>Poisson Solvers</span>
<span>Adaptive Mesh Refinement</span>

</div>

</div>

<div class="projects-vision">

<div class="projects-vision-label">RESEARCH VISION</div>

<h2>From Kinetic Physics to Scalable Simulation</h2>

<p>
Across these projects, we develop a common computational foundation based on
<strong>kinetic theory, particle simulation, and GPU-accelerated
high-performance computing</strong>.
</p>

<p>
The long-term goal is to build predictive simulation capabilities that connect
fundamental nonequilibrium physics with challenging problems in
<strong>plasma processing, semiconductor manufacturing, hypersonic
aerothermodynamics, and advanced scientific computing</strong>.
</p>
